## 9. Key Findings

1. **Developed countries hold 70.4% of all country-attributed DSI records** despite containing only 63 of 239 classified countries.

2. **The developed-country share declined from 74.2% in 2018 to 54.4% in 2025**, driven by rapid growth in China, India, Brazil, and other developing countries. However, the gap remains enormous in absolute terms.

3. **LDCs account for only 2.8% of country-attributed records** — the 47 least developed countries combined have fewer records than Canada alone (527,333 vs 530,725).

4. **A COVID-19 sequencing surge in 2021-2023** temporarily widened the gap: developed countries grew +212% (2018-2020 to 2021-2023 annual average), while developing countries grew +142%. This is now reversing.

5. **Europe dominates with 40.9% of records**, led by the United Kingdom (4,109,861 records, mostly from the COVID-19 Genomics UK consortium). Northern Europe alone has more records than all of Africa and Oceania combined.

6. **Upper middle income countries (led by China) are the fastest-growing group**, with 2024-2025 annualized output +55% above 2021-2023, while high-income countries declined -51%.

7. **Africa has only 3.6% of records** despite having 59 classified countries — the lowest regional share by far. Sub-Saharan Africa has 658,822 records total.

8. **36% of all non-human records cannot be attributed to any country** (oceans, seas, placeholders, GAZ identifiers, or no country field). This limits the utility of INSDC provenance data for country-level allocation methodologies.

9. **21.5% of records are undated** (no `first_public`), so annual counts are partial censuses. The dated share may vary by country, introducing temporal bias.

## 10. Caveats

- All counts are a point-in-time snapshot (2026-10-12); the ENA archive grows daily.
- Annual counts cover only the ~78% of records with a `first_public` date.
- `country` records collection locality, not sovereign provider of genetic resources.
- The United Kingdom count is inflated by the COVID-19 Genomics UK (COG-UK) consortium, which deposited millions of SARS-CoV-2 records in 2021-2023.
- The USA count is similarly influenced by large US sequencing centers during the pandemic.
- Environmental metagenomes (27.7% of non-human records) are included; they inflate counts for countries with large environmental sequencing programs.
- 17 collision exclusions (9 cross-country + 8 sub-national) are applied to prevent false matches; see methodology document for details.
- Historical names (USSR, Yugoslavia, etc.) with multiple successor states cannot be cleanly assigned and are excluded from country-level analysis.
- Columbia is mapped to Colombia but may include some British Columbia records not captured by the Canada exclusion.
